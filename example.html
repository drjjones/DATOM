Preliminary Confidence

Of the 130 studies that directly measure this claim (muscle mass or lean body mass during GLP-1 therapy in overweight or obese adults), 77 (59%) support it, 25 (19%) contradict it, and 28 report conditional findings tied to specific dose, population, or duration. That directional majority is real, but DATOM's confidence score of only 44% reflects a separate question: evidence quality, not direction. Statistical power is a material limitation across the corpus: a large share of studies with reported sample sizes are underpowered, and many more report no sample size at all, which limits how much weight any aggregate finding can bear. Evidence drawing on systems outside the target population (e.g. animal models) is included for context but should be read as adjacency evidence, not direct support.

Why 59% agreement isn't 59% confidence: The direct evidence leans toward this claim: 59% of the 130 directly-relevant studies support it, versus 19% that contradict it. But DATOM's confidence score (44%, “Preliminary”) measures evidence quality and internal coherence, not the direction of the finding — a majority pointing the same way does not by itself mean the evidence is strong enough to act on. Genuine uncertainty remains: statistical power is compromised across much of the corpus, cross-species findings cannot be directly transferred to the target population without independent validation, and a substantial number of directly-contradicting findings remain unresolved and warrant individual review before high-stakes citation.

Fault lines DATOM flagged in this evidence base: Quality-Integrity Mismatch: a meaningful share of studies score below acceptable methodological-quality thresholds, and no quality-based filtering has been applied to the raw counts above — when low-quality studies are weighted equally with high-quality ones, a directional majority becomes a weaker signal than it first appears. Unresolved Contradictions: a real minority of direct studies still contradict muscle-mass reduction outright or condition it on specific doses, populations, or durations — those findings haven't been reconciled, and treating the majority direction as settled would understate that disagreement.
